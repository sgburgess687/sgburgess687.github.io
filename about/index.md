        <h2>Mathematics first</h2>
        <p>
          I studied mathematics at the University of Houston, with an option in Mathematical
          Finance and a minor in economics — training that was less about any one industry and
          more about how to reason carefully from data to a decision. That way of thinking has
          carried through most of my work since graduating.
        </p>

        <h2>Into analytics and transportation</h2>
        <p>
          I spent the next three years as a data and analytics consultant, placed with United
          Airlines' Corporate Analytics group. The work ranged from migrating a legacy SQL Server
          database to Amazon Redshift, to defining check-in and service-exception metrics from raw
          kiosk log data, to expanding the ancillary revenue model with commercial stakeholders.
          Across those projects, the goal was usually the same: take messy operational data and
          turn it into something the business could actually use.
        </p>
        <p>
          Before that, I worked in data engineering for Smartbridge on a Rockcliff Energy
          engagement, and spent a few months in Marine Business Intelligence at Phillips&nbsp;66 —
          building Power BI and Tableau reporting for Marine business and accounting users. That
          last one mattered more than a typical rotation: it was my first direct look at how a
          transportation-and-logistics-driven business runs on its data, and it's a large part of
          why maritime is where I decided to go next.
        </p>

        <h2>Why maritime, deliberately</h2>
        <p>
          Maritime made sense to me because it brings together a lot of the work I'd already
          done: quantitative modeling, financial and operational reporting, and transportation
          systems. It's also an industry where there aren't many people who can do the analysis
          and understand the business at the same time, which felt like an opening. I'm not
          leaving analytics behind. I'm applying it to a narrower industry where I want to build
          a career.
        </p>

        <h2>Graduate Study at Texas A&amp;M Galveston</h2>
        <p>
          I'm currently completing a full-time, accelerated Master of Maritime Business
          Administration &amp; Logistics at Texas A&amp;M University at Galveston, expected
          December 2026. Coursework covers port operations and economics, maritime financial
          management, maritime business analytics and decision making, and logistics and supply
          chain management. I'm also a participant in Stolt-Nielsen's mentorship program,
          underway since September 2026.
        </p>
